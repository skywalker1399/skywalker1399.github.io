PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT

Professional Self-Assessment

A reflection on my growth throughout the Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University, the technical skills I have developed, and my capstone experience.

Read Full Self-Assessment +

Throughout my time in the Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University, I have developed a stronger understanding of software development, programming principles, problem-solving, and the importance of creating reliable and secure applications. The program has allowed me to gain experience with several programming languages, development tools, and software engineering practices. More importantly, it has taught me how to approach complex problems, evaluate existing solutions, and make improvements that increase the functionality, efficiency, and maintainability of software. My capstone experience has provided an opportunity to demonstrate these skills by revisiting previous projects and applying the knowledge I have gained throughout my education.

One of the most valuable skills I developed during the program was the ability to analyze and improve existing code. When I first began programming, my primary focus was creating applications that performed their intended functions. As I progressed through the program, I began to understand that successful software development involves much more than simply writing functional code. Software must also be organized, maintainable, efficient, secure, and understandable to other developers. Through coursework and practical programming assignments, I learned the importance of object-oriented programming, proper documentation, input validation, testing, and following established coding standards.

The Computer Science program also helped me develop my ability to communicate technical information effectively. Throughout my coursework, I was required to explain programming decisions, document software functionality, and evaluate different approaches to solving problems. These experiences helped me recognize the importance of communication and collaboration within software development. Developers must be able to explain their work to both technical and nontechnical audiences, particularly when working with teams or presenting solutions to stakeholders. By creating documentation, code reviews, and written reflections, I have strengthened my ability to communicate the reasoning behind my technical decisions.

Another important area of growth was my understanding of algorithms and data structures. I learned how selecting appropriate data structures and designing efficient algorithms can significantly affect an application's performance and functionality. My coursework provided experience working with arrays, vectors, hash tables, linked lists, and other data structures. I also gained experience developing algorithms that organize, search, and process information. These skills have improved my ability to evaluate problems and select solutions based on the specific requirements of an application.

Security has also become an important consideration in my approach to software development. Throughout the program, I learned that developers must anticipate potential vulnerabilities rather than treating security as something to address only after an application has been completed. Practices such as validating user input, protecting sensitive information, and using secure database operations are essential for creating trustworthy software. I have developed a greater appreciation for defensive programming and the importance of identifying weaknesses during the design and development process.

My capstone project allowed me to demonstrate these skills through three enhancements focused on software design and engineering, algorithms and data structures, and databases.

Software Design and Engineering: Investment Calculator

For my first enhancement, I revisited a C++ investment calculator that originally calculated investment growth based on user-provided financial information. I improved the application by implementing a more organized object-oriented structure, adding the ability to compare two investment plans, strengthening input validation, and incorporating automated testing. These improvements demonstrated my ability to modify existing software while maintaining its original functionality. The automated tests also allowed me to verify the accuracy of financial calculations and identify errors during development. This project represents my growth in software engineering practices, code organization, and application reliability.

Algorithms and Data Structures: Course Planner

My second enhancement focused on a C++ course planning application that used a hash table to store and retrieve course information. I expanded the application by allowing users to track completed courses, identify remaining prerequisites, and generate a course plan based on prerequisite relationships. This required me to develop additional algorithms that evaluated course dependencies and determined an appropriate order for completing courses. I also improved the hash function to distribute course information more effectively throughout the hash table. This enhancement demonstrates my understanding of data structures, algorithmic problem-solving, and the importance of designing software that addresses practical user needs.

Databases: Text-Based Game

For my third enhancement, I improved a Python text-based game by integrating an SQLite relational database. The original application relied on Python dictionaries to manage rooms, items, and player information. I replaced much of this functionality with database tables that stored room connections, items, player records, game sessions, and inventory information. I also implemented functionality that allowed players to save their progress and return to their games without losing their information. Throughout development, I used parameterized SQL queries to improve the security of database interactions. This project strengthened my understanding of relational database design, data persistence, SQL operations, and the integration of databases with software applications.

Reflection and Professional Growth

Completing these enhancements presented several challenges. I encountered problems involving incorrect calculations, application logic, data retrieval, and database relationships. Resolving these issues required debugging, testing, researching potential solutions, and carefully evaluating how changes affected other parts of the applications. These experiences reinforced the importance of patience, attention to detail, and a systematic approach to problem-solving. They also demonstrated how much my ability to independently identify and resolve programming issues has improved since beginning the program.

The three projects included in this ePortfolio represent different areas of computer science while demonstrating a common goal: improving software through thoughtful design, efficient problem-solving, and secure development practices. Together, they highlight my ability to work with C++, Python, and SQL, as well as my experience with object-oriented programming, algorithms, relational databases, automated testing, and software documentation.

As I prepare to enter the next stage of my professional career, I believe the knowledge and experience gained through the Computer Science program have provided a strong foundation for continued growth in software development. I understand that computer science is a constantly evolving field, and I intend to continue learning new technologies, improving my programming abilities, and applying industry best practices. My goal is to contribute to the development of reliable, efficient, and secure software solutions that provide meaningful value to users and organizations.

This ePortfolio represents both the technical skills I have developed and my growth as a problem-solver throughout the program. It demonstrates my ability to evaluate existing applications, identify opportunities for improvement, and implement solutions using the principles and practices I have learned during my education.
